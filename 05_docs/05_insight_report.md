## Three Things BCPL Must Know

**1. The procurement problem is a supplier problem — and a planning problem.**
Three import suppliers (MegaChem Corp, GlobalPack Imports, AsiaFood Commodities) account for 59% of total procurement leakage through consistent price overcharging. But the data also reveals that emergency orders carry a 20.8% average price variance versus just 3.3% for regular orders — meaning poor procurement planning is making the supplier problem significantly worse.

**2. Dead stock is a product problem, not a warehouse problem.**
23.3% of BCPL's inventory is dead stock with an average age of 126 days. Packaged Food alone drives 49.88% of dead stock leakage across all four warehouses — which means the root cause is upstream demand forecasting and SKU planning, not warehouse operations.

**3. Sylhet is a recurring risk signal across the entire supply chain.**
Sylhet Regional Depot holds the highest inventory leakage (৳3.61 lakh). The Sylhet Tea Belt route has the worst late delivery rate (40%) and highest freight overrun (32.83%). Sylhet appears as a high-risk area across Inventory, Logistics, and Root Cause dashboards — pointing to a deeper regional supply chain misalignment.
